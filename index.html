ABOUT ME

Watching enzymes think: motion, gating, and binding at atomic resolution

I am a doctoral candidate in the Department of Chemical Engineering at Indian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar, India, under the supervision of Prof. Mithun Radhakrishna.

Enzymes are never still: loops flex, gates open and shut, and a small change in shape decides which molecule binds. I capture these motions, from enzyme-ligand binding to protein folding, by combining simulations, free-energy calculations, Markov State Models, and machine learning with experiments such as isothermal titration calorimetry.
The goal: biosensors for environmental pollutant detection and enzymes for bioremediation.

My journey into the molecular world began with a B.Tech. in Biotechnology Engineering at NIT Durgapur, India (2021). Besides being an academician, I am a music vocalist and a 1st Dan Black Belt Karateka in the Shito-Ryu style.

Research Projects

ENZYME DYNAMICS

Conformational Gating in Fungal Laccase

Investigated substrate selectivity in laccase through molecular dynamics simulations, Markov State Models, and isothermal titration calorimetry. Identified conformational gating mechanism governed by flexible active-site loop that differentially regulates phenolic and aromatic amine substrate binding.

BIOSENSING APPLICATIONS

Catechol Binding Mechanism to Laccase

Reconstructed complete binding mechanism of catechol to laccase using MD simulations, free-energy calculations, and transition path theory. Identified five distinct macrostates and two dominant binding pathways for biosensor design applications.

Read Publication →
PROTEIN AGGREGATION

Computational Approaches to Protein Aggregation

Comprehensive review of advanced computational approaches to understand protein aggregation mechanisms, folding pathways, and stability effects.

PROTEIN FOLDING & STABILITY

Mutational Effects on γD-Crystallin Folding and Stability

Used all-atom molecular dynamics simulations to identify the residues that stabilize the motifs and domains of γD-crystallin, a protein whose aggregation causes cataracts. Showed that point mutations to aspartic and glutamic acid destabilize and unfold individual domains, shedding light on its domain-swapping aggregation mechanism.
